OPEN MISSION BRIEF — Problem Statement, Objectives and Scoring

The Emergency: One City. Many Decisions.

It is a busy working day in Howrah. The emergency control room receives a report of a fire near a densely used urban area. The report is fictional, but the geographic challenge is realistic.

Fire stations, hospitals, educational institutions, petrol pumps, roads and water bodies are distributed across the city. Some facilities are close to the incident; others may be easier or harder to reach.

Your team has access to a digital intelligence map. However, the map does not tell you everything. It does not know current traffic, actual fire-engine availability, hospital capacity or whether a road is blocked.

Your challenge: Make the best defensible emergency-response recommendation, identify risks, and explain what additional information you would need before sending responders.

MISSION 1 — Locate

Identify the simulated incident and examine the surrounding area. What makes this location potentially vulnerable?

MISSION 2 — Rescue Intelligence

Identify two candidate fire stations and one candidate hospital. Explain why each deserves consideration.

MISSION 3 — Hidden Risks

Investigate nearby schools, fuel stations, water bodies and roads. Which mapped features could affect the response?

MISSION 4 — The Decision

Recommend a response strategy and a possible access corridor. Explain what cannot be confirmed from the map alone.

BONUS — The Twist

Suppose the most obvious access road is blocked. How would you reconsider your response? What evidence would you request?

What Your Team Must Submit

Prepare a short evidence-based briefing containing:

  1. The incident location and nearby risk factors.
  2. Two candidate fire stations and one hospital.
  3. A proposed access approach, clearly marked as unverified.
  4. Three reasons supporting your decision.
  5. Two critical pieces of missing information.
  6. Your answer to the blocked-road challenge.

Scientific Thinking Challenge

Physics: How could road width, travel speed and congestion influence response time?

Chemistry: Why might a fuel station near a fire require special precautions?

Biology: Why do hospitals, population exposure and vulnerable people matter?

Mathematics: Why is straight-line distance different from road-network distance?

Geospatial Intelligence: How can all these observations be combined into one defensible decision?
