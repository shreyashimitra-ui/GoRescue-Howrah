One Emergency. Multiple Possible Decisions.

Imagine a fire breaks out during a busy working day in Howrah. A caller reports smoke and confusion, but the emergency control room has no confirmed information about traffic, available fire engines or hospital capacity.

You have a map containing fire stations, hospitals, roads, bridges, schools, fuel stations, police facilities, water bodies and open spaces. Can your team identify the most defensible response plan before the other teams?

The twist: The nearest facility may not be the best choice. Straight-line distance does not equal driving time, and a mapped road is not proof that it is currently passable.

MISSION 1 · The Scene

Locate the pulsing incident marker. Identify three mapped features that could influence the emergency.

MISSION 2 · First Responders

Choose two candidate fire stations and one hospital. Defend your choices using the map.

MISSION 3 · Hidden Danger

Check for fuel stations, schools, road crossings, bridges and nearby open spaces. Which deserve attention?

MISSION 4 · Your Strategy

Suggest a possible access approach and list two facts you must verify before dispatch.

BONUS · The Roadblock

Your preferred road is suddenly closed. How would you adapt, and what new information would you request?

What Your Team Must Present

  1. Incident location and three important surrounding features.
  2. Two candidate fire stations and one candidate hospital.
  3. Your proposed response approach, clearly labelled as unverified.
  4. Three evidence-based reasons for your recommendation.
  5. Two crucial missing pieces of information.
  6. Your alternative plan if a road is blocked.

Competition Score · 100 Points

Observation: 20 · Facility choice: 20 · Risk recognition: 20 · Reasoning: 20 · Roadblock response: 10 · Presentation: 10.

Scientific connection: Physics explains travel time and access; chemistry explains fuel hazards; biology explains human exposure and medical needs; mathematics helps compare distance; geospatial science connects the evidence.

Important: Parks and open spaces are not automatically approved evacuation shelters. The red circle is an illustrative investigation area, not a measured hazard zone.
